# MUSINIQUE — the brand charter

**The one-sentence truth: Musinique points the same tools the platforms use
against you — back at you, in your favor.**

This channel fights for the indie musician. Not "supports." Not "celebrates."
Fights — because the independent musician is currently outgunned by two armies
of machines: the corporate bots (recommendation engines tuned for the catalog
majors, playlist pipelines with tolls at every gate, engagement metrics that
reward the already-large) and the scam bots (pay-for-playlist rackets,
guaranteed-placement services, stream farms that take an indie's $300 and
return numbers that get their catalog flagged). Both armies profit from the
same belief: that the musician cannot operate the machinery themselves.

Musinique exists to end that belief. Every video teaches the indie to use
bots to fight the corporate bots — and to walk past the scammers entirely,
because the musician who can roll their own promotion deck has nothing to
buy from people selling promises.

## The register — Baldwin

Musinique narrates in the Baldwin register: testimony, not content. First
person that takes responsibility for the analysis. Direct address — you, the

evidence, then the short one that lands it. Moral interrogation over
description: don't just explain the playlist scam — ask who built the

toll booth and called it discovery*) · adjectives as argument · hedged takes.
A Musinique video that hedges is a Musinique video that lied about which side
it's on.

## The four pillars (touch at least two per video)

1. **The tools are not the point. The intent is everything.** The same
   generative and analytical machinery serves the label's A&R sweep and the
   indie's Friday release. The tool is neutral; the pointing is not.
2. **Music is a neurological technology — and it belongs to everyone.**
   The promotion machine treats songs as inventory. We treat them as spells
   that work on human nervous systems — which is exactly why they're worth
   fighting for.
3. **We build in public and we show our work.** Every claim carries a
   receipt. Every workflow we teach is one we run ourselves. The beat sheets,
   gates, and decision logs ship beside the videos.
4. **The margin is the center.** The indie with 400 monthly listeners is not
   a lead to be qualified. They are the audience, the client, and the point.

## The laws

**1. RECEIPTS LAW.** Let data indict. A video may accuse a *mechanism* (how
pay-for-playlist works, how stream farms poison catalogs, how "guaranteed
placement" launders bot traffic) with mechanism-level evidence. It may accuse
a *named company* only with primary sources on screen. No source, no verdict —
the Claude, Debunked? rule, applied to an industry that runs on unverifiable
promises. We never become the thing we're grading.

**2. NO FAKE NUMBERS, EVER.** The scam economy's product is counterfeit
proof — bought streams, bought saves, bought followers. Musinique never buys,
fakes, or inflates a number, including its own. Our numbers appear with their
context or they don't appear. Fluency isn't mastery; streams aren't listeners.

**3. ROLL-YOUR-OWN LAW (the handoff, weaponized).** Every video's handoff
builds one working piece of the viewer's own promotion deck — a paste-ready
prompt that generates their release plan, their pitch email, their reel
beat-sheet, their playlist-scam detector — runnable in their own Claude or
CLI that day, for free. The series' arc is cumulative: a viewer who follows
the handoffs ends the season owning a complete promo machine they were
otherwise going to rent from a stranger. We don't sell the deck. We teach
the rolling.

**4. BOT-VS-BOT LAW.** When a video teaches defense (spotting a scam, auditing
a curator, checking a service's claims), it teaches it as automation the
musician runs — "paste this, and let your bot read their promises" — because
the scammer is counting on the indie being too busy to check. The indie's
scarcest resource is time; our job is giving their machines the vigilance.

**5. THE GRADED EXHIBIT.** The signature visual move: put the artifact on
screen — the DM from the "curator," the promo service's pricing page, the
label's playlist pitch — and grade it, receipt by receipt, the way
claude-oversimplified graded the poster and claude-debunked graded the wheel.
The exhibit keeps its own look (it is evidence, not a brand surface); the
grading happens in ours.

**6. HONOR GRIEF WITHOUT PERFORMING IT.** Musinique's catalog includes ghost
artists and Spirit Songs — music made for the dead, the missing, the small
hours. When these enter a video, they enter as fact and testimony, never as
emotional bait. The engagement-trap law applies to feelings too.

**7. NEVER.** Sound like a tech startup that discovered social impact.
"Disruptive," "innovative," "game-changing." AI as the hero — AI is the tool;
the musician is the hero. Overclaiming early-stage research. Begging ("if you
enjoyed this, consider…" — no: *subscribe*).

corpus article, and the article's own citations ride along. The scout skill
mines this folder; reels land in `books/musinique/youtube/`.

conform-to-audio, GATE P human sign-off before paid voice spend, decision

named what we're seeing? Would the musician in the van, watching on a phone
at 1 a.m., leave with one more working tool and one less illusion? If not,
revise toward honesty.**
