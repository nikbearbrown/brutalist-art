# Muybridge Blueprint Template

Use this template for short textbook concept videos. Keep it source-grounded and learner-centered.

## Concept

One sentence:

Audience:

Learner already knows:

Learner is likely confused by:

Include:

Exclude:

Tutorial shape:

## Blueprint

### Slide 1

**Purpose:** Hook the misconception, puzzle, or practical need.

**Visual intent:** One focused image, contrast, or question-state.

**On-screen text:** 3-8 words, optional.

**Speaker notes:**
Open with the learner's problem. Do not define everything yet.

---

### Slide 2

**Purpose:** Name the concept and set the frame.

**Visual intent:** Show the main actor, system, or relationship.

**On-screen text:** Concept name.

**Speaker notes:**
Define the concept in ordinary language, grounded in the source.

---

### Slide 3

**Purpose:** Show the first mechanism or distinction.

**Visual intent:** Diagram, before/after, timeline, or two-panel comparison.

**On-screen text:** One label per major part.

**Speaker notes:**
Explain one move. Avoid side paths.

---

### Slide 4

**Purpose:** Make the invisible relationship visible.

**Visual intent:** Animate cause/effect, mapping, constraint, or feedback.

**On-screen text:** Optional key phrase.

**Speaker notes:**
Connect the pieces. Use a concrete example if the source supports it.

---

### Slide 5

**Purpose:** Repair the most likely misconception.

**Visual intent:** Wrong model fades or is crossed out; better model replaces it.

**On-screen text:** "Not X. Y."

**Speaker notes:**
Name the trap and the correction.

---

### Slide 6

**Purpose:** Transfer the concept to a new example or test.

**Visual intent:** Small worked example, prediction, or application.

**On-screen text:** Short prompt or result.

**Speaker notes:**
Ask the learner to predict, then reveal the answer.

---

### Slide 7

**Purpose:** Close with the durable mental model.

**Visual intent:** Return to the opening image, now resolved.

**On-screen text:** One memorable sentence fragment.

**Speaker notes:**
Give the learner the sentence they should remember.
