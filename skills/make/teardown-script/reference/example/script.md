# Why a Cooled Electron Never Stops

**Key case (not spoken):** Cool a trapped electron toward absolute zero — it
still jitters. Why won't it stop?

**Length:** ~63 s ≈ 1.1 min · **Tier:** Single-insight (1 abstraction, 2 instances)
**Register:** Teardown · **Pedagogy:** Brown Blue

---

**B01 · HOOK** — Cool this trapped electron toward absolute zero. Everything else
stops moving. It doesn't.
*[visual: an electron in a box; a thermometer falls to ~0 K; the particle keeps shaking]*

**B02 · INSTANCE** — Squeeze the box narrower and the slowest wiggle it can hold
gets faster — not slower.
*[visual: well width L animates smaller; the lowest standing wave speeds up]*

**B03 · INSTANCE** — Widen it and that same lowest wiggle calms down — but it
never flattens to a straight line.
*[visual: L animates wider; amplitude drops but never reaches zero]*

**B04 · TRANSFORM** — Watch why: pin both ends to the walls and you are forced to
bend the wave, and bending costs motion.
*[visual: the same wave morphing as the walls move; endpoints stay pinned at zero]*

**B05 · ABSTRACTION** *(lands an equation → TANGENT next)* — So the smallest
allowed energy is set by how tightly you confine it: E-one goes as one over
L-squared.
*[on screen: E₁ = π²ħ² / (2 m L²)]*

**B06 · TANGENT** — Read the symbols: L is the box width, m the mass. Smaller
box, higher floor. That is the whole claim.
*[visual: each symbol labeled in plain words; the 1/L² term highlighted]*

**B07 · PAYOFF** *(returns the HOOK object)* — That is why the cooled electron
never rests: stopping would pin it to one spot, and confinement forbids it.
*[on screen: E₁ > 0]*

**B08 · BOUNDARY / OUTRO** — We did not build the higher rungs or prove the bound
— that is the next video. Your turn.
**Viewer exercise:** Compute E₁ for L = 1 nm and L = 2 nm and confirm the energy
quadruples when L halves.
