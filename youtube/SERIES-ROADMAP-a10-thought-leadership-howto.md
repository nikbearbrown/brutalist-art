## The strategic frame (hold it all series)

- **Owned beats borrowed.** A9 published on LinkedIn — a borrowed platform. This is a HOME: a
  publication you own, a subscriber list you control, a voice that compounds over time. Say why
  that matters (indexed, shareable, builds reputation while you sleep).
- **This deploys A9 and A7–8.** The article draws on the A9 story arcs / expanded narrative; the
  Substack look uses the A7–8 visual brand assets. Every episode says "pull from A9 / A7–8."

## Honesty / craft threads (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW) — thought leadership lives or dies on these

- **The best self-promotion never feels like self-promotion.** The article is a demonstration of
  thinking, not a product announcement — it must give the reader something genuinely useful (a
  framework, an insight, a perspective they didn't have). Say it every relevant episode.
- **The tool is referenced NATURALLY — not forced, not buried.** A named grading line (Madison
  Tool Integration, 10 pts). The video shows the difference between a natural mention and an ad.
- **Published LIVE, not a draft.** Real Substack URL, real LinkedIn post. A draft in a PDF doesn't
  count.
- **Headline read-aloud test** — "would you click it?" (`skep:subject`). Same gut-check honesty as
  the UVP.
- **Ship it: published and imperfect beats perfect and invisible.** The 80/20 ethos — the skill is
  earning attention you were never promised, so don't polish into paralysis.
- **Claude structures; the final voice is yours** — the human-in-the-loop / +1 thread, one more time.

## The episodes (build in this order)

### E1 — "Own Your Home: Launch a Brand-Aligned Substack (Part 1 setup, 10 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a10-01-launch-substack`
- **premise:** A borrowed platform can drop you; a home can't. Set up a Substack that looks like
  it belongs to your brand family.
- **teach:** why owned > borrowed (compounds, indexed, subscriber list you control); create the
  publication; a **name** (personal or tool brand), a **tagline** (one sharp sentence: exactly what
  readers get), an **About page** (150–200 words), a **logo/header** from the A7–8 visual assets,
  and the **custom URL** (`yourname.substack.com`). "Same brand family as your website and LinkedIn
  — clearly related, not identical." Introduce `skep:about` / `skep:manifesto`.
- **traps:** off-brand default template; a vague tagline; no custom URL.
- **deliverable:** the live Substack publication. Your Turn = `skep:about` (or a prompt) that drafts
  the 150–200 word About page from the A6 brand + A9 story.

### E2 — "The Brand Consistency Audit (Part 1 audit, 10 pts)" (~4 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a10-02-brand-audit`
- **premise:** Prove the three surfaces feel like one brand — with evidence, not assertion.
- **teach:** the **screenshot comparison** — Substack header / LinkedIn header / website hero —
  side by side; a **3–5 sentence explanation** of how they cohere (shared palette, type, logo,
  voice — from A7–8) while not being identical. Assemble as a one-page PDF.
- **traps:** three surfaces that don't match; "it's cohesive" with no evidence.
- **deliverable:** the brand-consistency audit PDF. Your Turn = a prompt that drafts the cohesion
  explanation from the A7–8 style guide once the student pastes what each surface uses.

### E3 — "Write Thought Leadership That Doesn't Feel Like Self-Promo (Part 2 article, craft)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a10-03-write-thought-leadership`
- **premise:** The article has to do three things at once — establish you as credible, introduce
  the tool, and give the reader something genuinely useful. If it reads like an ad, it failed.
- **teach:** the three simultaneous jobs; pick the **angle** (A: your POV on an industry problem /
  a lesson others can apply / why the conventional approach is broken / how AI changes your work —
  B: the problem told as a story / a counterintuitive truth / a framework your tool is built on /
  what surprised you building it); the **opening hook** (the first two sentences must earn the
  third); build the piece around a **framework or insight** the reader keeps. Draw on the A9 arc.
  `skep:essay` / `skep:take` as the drafting engine.
- **traps:** a product announcement; no useful takeaway; a hook that doesn't hook.
- **deliverable:** the 600–800 word article draft. Your Turn = `skep:take` (or a prompt) that turns
  the A9 arc + a real insight into a thought-leadership draft, then the voice pass.

### E4 — "Headline, Visual, Natural Tool Mention, CTA — Then Publish (Part 2 article, ship)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a10-04-headline-visual-publish`
- **premise:** A great draft still needs a headline that earns the click, a visual that carries the
  brand, and a tool mention that feels earned — then it goes LIVE.
- **teach:** the **headline** (read-aloud "would you click it?" — `skep:subject`); **at least one
  custom visual** (tool screenshot, branded graphic, process diagram, or data viz in Canva/Figma/
  Adobe Express — on-brand); reference the **Madison tool naturally** (woven in where it adds value,
  not bolted on); professional formatting (subheads, short paragraphs, scannable); a clear **CTA**
  (subscribe / try the tool / connect / comment); **publish live** and grab the URL.
- **traps:** a flat headline; generic stock image; a forced/buried tool mention; no CTA; left as a draft.
- **deliverable:** the live Substack article + visual. Your Turn = a prompt that drafts three
  headline options (read-aloud tested) and a natural one-line tool mention.

### E5 — "Promote, Reflect & Ship (Part 2 promo 10 + Part 3 reflection 20 + submission)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a10-05-promote-reflect-ship`
- **premise:** Publishing is half the job — get the click, then prove you were thinking
  strategically, then submit.
- **teach:** the **LinkedIn promotion post** (2–4 sentences that give value or tease the key
  insight — not "I wrote a thing"; link + a graphic from the article; screenshot the post with any
  engagement); the **300-word reflection** (strategic choices of angle/tone/structure and why; how
  this positions you differently; what you'd do differently; one sentence on the next piece — this
  is how they know you thought strategically). Then assemble the single **PDF** (Substack link,
  article link, brand audit, LinkedIn screenshot, 300-word reflection) and submit to Canvas.
- **traps:** "check out my article" caption; a mechanical reflection; missing a required PDF element.
- **deliverable:** the promo post + reflection + submitted PDF. Your Turn = `skep:preview` (or a
  prompt) that drafts a value-first LinkedIn caption from the article's sharpest line.
