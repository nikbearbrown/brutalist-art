## Series doctrine (every episode)

- **Everything lands on ONE Figma board.** The whole assignment is a single board with six
  sections. Every episode ends by adding its section to that board, not a loose file.
- **Assume zero prior tools.** Name every button; define every term the first time (FigJam,
  ATS, orchestration layer, custom URL, repo/README). Never "just."
- **The AI drafts, the student owns the truth.** Every episode shows the exact prompt to get
  a first draft (intro copy, proposal, goals), then what to CHECK and personalize. AI is the
  scaffold; the student's real background is the content.
- **One board-section per episode = one Your Turn** (the deliverable), with the paste-ready
  prompt that starts it.
- **ai-explainer bookends stay:** composer cold open → step-by-step body → verdict recap →
  Your Turn (the section) → title-restate outro. 16:9. Free pipeline (Kokoro). Register
  metadata: `"register": "Pragmatist"`.
- **Honesty:** tool UIs (Figma, GitHub, LinkedIn, Jobscan) and the Madison repo change — each
  SOURCES.md notes "re-verify the current UI + repo before recording." No lifted screenshots
  (REBUILD LAW): flows are rebuilt as clean illustrated steps. The Madison facts below are
  from its live README (verified 2026-07-18) and must be re-checked at build time.

## The episodes (build in this order)

### E1 — "Start Here: One Board, Six Sections" (~3 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-01-the-board`
- **premise:** Assignment 1 looks like six tasks; it's one Figma board. See the whole shape
  before touching anything so you don't freeze.
- **teach:** what FigJam is (define it); sign up free with your **NEU email** (3 free boards);
  create the board; lay out six labeled sections up front (Intro · Skills · Madison Proposal ·
  Baseline+Goals · 4 Semester Goals · Tools Setup); set consistent styling now so the grade's
  "clear organization / consistent visual styling" points are banked early; how to get the
  **shareable link with view/comment** at the end.
- **deliverable produced:** the empty, well-organized board. Your Turn = "make your NEU Figma
  account and drop in the six section headers before your next class."

### E2 — "Your Professional Introduction Card (5 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-02-intro-card`
- **premise:** The intro card is you in one glance — background, where you're headed, and the
  one thing only you bring.
- **teach (the five required pieces):** current background/experience; 2–3 year career vision;
  why AI + branding; your one unique perspective/skill; 3–5 skills/technologies. The **pro-tip
  rule on camera:** write skills as "Machine Learning (ML)" — full term AND acronym, so the
  materials work everywhere. Then design it as a card in FigJam: professional photo/avatar,
  brand-ish styling, "balance creativity with clarity." Show the AI prompt that drafts the copy
  from the student's raw notes, then the "make it sound like you, not a template" pass.
- **beginner traps:** wall of text; no photo; buzzwords with no plain-language version; generic
  "passionate about technology."
- **deliverable produced:** the intro card section. Your Turn = the paste-ready "draft my
  professional intro from these notes" prompt.

### E3 — "Skills Inventory + Market Awareness (5 pts)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-03-skills-inventory`
- **premise:** This isn't a brag sheet — it's an honest 1–5 self-rating checked against what
  real job posts actually ask for.
- **teach:** the four skill buckets, rate 1–5 honestly (AI Tools · Design & Branding · Technical ·
  Professional); how to build the **visualization** in FigJam (simple bar/radar with sticky
  notes — no fancy tool needed); the market-awareness step done properly — **pull 3–5 real job
  postings** in the target field, note which skills repeat, separate technical vs soft; then pick
  **3 areas to strengthen** this semester and say WHY they matter for the career goal.
- **beginner traps:** rating everything a 5; skipping the job-posting research; growth areas with
  no rationale.
- **deliverable produced:** the skills-visualization + improvement-areas section. Your Turn = a
  prompt that takes 3 pasted job descriptions and extracts the most-frequent skills to compare
  against their self-rating.

### E4 — "The Madison Framework Proposal (5 pts)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-04-madison-proposal`
- **premise:** You're going to extend a real open-source AI marketing framework. First you have
  to read enough of it to pick one honest, doable piece.
- **teach:** what Madison is — an **open-source, agent-based AI marketing intelligence framework**
  (github.com/Humanitariansai/Madison); its shape at a glance — **five agent layers**
  (Intelligence, Content, Research, Experience, Performance) under an **orchestration layer**;
  Python/TypeScript stack. How to actually read a repo as a beginner: README first, then the
  examples/components, not the whole codebase. Pick ONE component to extend/improve; then write
  the **150–200 word proposal** answering the four questions (what · why valuable · skills gained ·
  real marketing/branding application) using **action verbs** (developed, implemented, designed —
  the portfolio-thinking tip, on camera). Show the drafting prompt, then the honesty pass ("only
  claim what you'll actually build").
- **beginner traps:** proposing to "rebuild the whole thing"; vague scope; passive voice; no tie
  to a real skill or use case.
- **deliverable produced:** the proposal section. Your Turn = a prompt that turns "here's the
  component I picked and why it interests me" into a tight 150–200 word action-verb proposal.
- **honesty note:** re-open the live README before recording — the repo evolves; confirm the
  agent layers and any component names shown on screen.

### E5 — "Your Brand Baseline + 4 Semester Goals (5 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-05-baseline-goals`
- **premise:** You can't measure growth without a starting photo. Screenshot where you are today,
  then set four goals you can actually check in week 12.
- **teach:** the current-state capture — **screenshot your LinkedIn profile**, note any existing
  portfolio/site, write your current professional story in a few lines, mark what to enhance; the
  professional-standards awareness beats stated plainly (resumes must work for **humans AND ATS** —
  define ATS; portfolios must be creative AND technical; consistency across platforms; keywords vs
  experience). Then the **four specific goals**, one each: Technical (a Madison contribution — reuse
  E4), Brand, Portfolio, Network (name who you want to connect with). Make each goal concrete and
  checkable, not a vibe.
- **beginner traps:** no baseline screenshot; goals like "get better at branding" (unmeasurable);
  four goals that are really one.
- **deliverable produced:** the baseline + goals section. Your Turn = a prompt that turns rough
  ambitions into four specific, checkable semester goals.

### E6 — "Tools Setup, Done Right (5 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-06-tools-setup`
- **premise:** Five points for making accounts — but the real win is doing it with ONE consistent
  professional identity so every platform reinforces the same you.
- **teach:** the required set and what each is FOR, so it's not busywork — Design/Dev: **Figma**
  (join the class workspace), **Adobe Creative Cloud** (free for NEU students), **GitHub** (for
  Madison); Professional: **LinkedIn** — and actually claim your **custom URL** (linkedin.com/in/
  yourname, show how), **Jobscan.co** free account (for later resume testing), a **ChatGPT or
  Claude** account; recommended: **Canva**, a portfolio platform (v0/Framer/Wix). The rule that
  earns the points: **consistent professional usernames across platforms.** Screenshot each setup,
  drop them in the Tools section.
- **beginner traps:** random handles per site; skipping the LinkedIn custom URL; no screenshots;
  forgetting to actually JOIN the class Figma workspace.
- **deliverable produced:** the tools-confirmation section — and now the whole board is complete.
  Your Turn = "claim your custom LinkedIn URL and pick one username you'll use everywhere; here's
  a prompt to check that handle's availability across the platforms."

### E7 — "Ship the Board: Export the PDF + Share the Link" (~3 min) — optional closer
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a1-07-submit`
- **premise:** The work is done; don't lose points on submission mechanics.
- **teach:** the final board pass against the grade (section organization, consistent styling,
  professional presentation); set the **shareable link to view/comment**; export the board to
  **PDF**; submit both. A 60-second "does it read as one cohesive board" check.
- **deliverable produced:** the submitted PDF + link. Your Turn = "share your board link with a
  classmate and ask them the one thing that's unclear — fix that before you submit."
