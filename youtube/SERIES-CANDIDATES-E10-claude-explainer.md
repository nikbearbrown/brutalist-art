# claude-explainer — the chapter harvest (E10+)

Scouted from the chapters/ folders of the twelve *claude* books. Every card
traces to a chapter; premises quote the source's own strongest move. These
slot in AFTER the theory arc (E5–E9 in SERIES-ROADMAP); order below is
recommended release order. Deduped against existing youtube/ reels and the
E5–E9 slate. All default-Bear channel unless noted. Laws apply to every card:
cold open on the composer, spark lines, one-page verdict artifact,
title-restate outro, hello-lexicon greeting.

## E10 — Claude, Chosen.
- **slug:** `claude-chosen` · Wagwan: 7 → hello: **"Ciao, Bear"** (Italian)
- **source:** claude/chapters/01-the-work-chooses-the-tool.md
- **premise:** The book's opening scene is the whole video: a nonprofit PM,
  three months of daily Claude, pastes client case summaries into chat —
  never asking whether the surface fit the task. "She reached for the
  familiar surface before she asked what the task actually required."
- **thesis line:** "You don't choose the tool. The work does."
- **cold open:** `which Claude do I even use for this?` — and the tab pill
  (Chat · Cowork · Code) is the literal set; callouts land on the tabs.
- **verdict page:** "The five questions — one page" (output type, context
  source, risk, reversibility, verification path — the chapter's routing dimensions).
- **why now:** it's the natural E10 — after the theory arc, the first
  practical question a new viewer has is *where do I type*.

## E11 — Claude, Briefed.
- **slug:** `claude-briefed` · Wagwan: 8 → hello: **"Shalom, Bear"** (Hebrew)
- **source:** claude-cowork/chapters/04-the-cowork-task-brief.md
- **premise:** "A task brief is not a longer prompt — it's the specification
  that makes your intent executable and inspectable." The chapter's warning is
  the hook: vague instruction + broad access = fluent, plausible, wrong.
- **thesis line:** "Vague plus powerful equals confidently wrong."
- **cold open:** a deliberately BAD ask types into the composer
  (`summarize the reports and make it good`) — then gets rewritten on screen.
- **verdict page:** "The seven fields — one page" (objective, sources,
  exclusions, artifact, decision rules, checkpoints, stop conditions).
- **note:** the composer-as-set has never been more literal — this is the
  episode about what belongs in that box.

## E12 — Claude, Promoted.
- **slug:** `claude-promoted` · Wagwan: 1 → hello: **"Namaste, Bear"** (Hindi)
- **source:** claude-agentic-ai/chapters/01-chatbot-assistant-agent.md
- **premise:** Same question, three systems, identical text — different KIND.
  The chapter's killer line: in the third scenario, "before she reviewed the
  diff, the codebase had changed." Chatbot → assistant → agent is a ladder of
  when things change in the world.
- **thesis line:** "The question isn't what it said. It's what it did — and when."
- **cold open:** `what's the difference between a chatbot and an agent?`
- **verdict page:** "The ladder — one page" (text you act on / text you apply /
  actions you review).
- **note:** one Onda code-block beat earns its place: the same bug asked three
  ways, the third one showing the diff already applied.

## E13 — Claude, Constituted.
- **slug:** `claude-constituted` · Wagwan: 3 → hello: **"Yassou, Bear"** (Greek)
- **source:** claude-code-for-teachers/chapters/03-claude-md.md
- **premise:** "CLAUDE.md is the file Claude reads at the start of every
  session — the difference between a Claude that knows your project and a
  Claude that guesses." The chapter's own taxonomy is the spine: CLAUDE.md =
  always-on rules · Skills = on-demand playbooks · Hooks = deterministic enforcement.
- **thesis line:** "One file, read first, every session. Write it like a constitution."
- **cold open:** `why does Claude keep forgetting how this project works?`
- **verdict page:** "Always-on, on-demand, enforced — one page."
- **note:** the direct sequel to E5 (Claude, Taught) — skills got their episode;
  this is the layer above and below them. Code-block beat shows a real five-element CLAUDE.md.

## E14 — Claude, Half-Right.
- **slug:** `claude-half-right` · Wagwan: 5 → hello: **"Aloha, Bear"** (Hawaiian)
- **source:** claude-code-for-teachers/chapters/09-dangerous-middle.md (+ students/09)
- **premise:** The dangerous middle: output that "passes every handoff
  condition you wrote and is still pedagogically wrong." Right and wrong
  simultaneously — the condition you did not know to write.
- **thesis line:** "The dangerous ones aren't the failures. They're the passes."
- **cold open:** `it passed all my checks — why is it still wrong?`
- **verdict page:** "Beyond the checklist — one page" (technical checks pass ≠
  judgment passes; where plausibility auditing must substitute).
- **note:** the practical shadow of E9 (Claude, Judged) — E9 says who checks
  what; this shows the case where the determinable checks all pass and the
  judgeable still fails.

## E15 — Claude, Confident.
- **slug:** `claude-confident` · Wagwan: 1 → hello: **"Privet, Bear"** (Russian)
- **source:** claude-agentic-ai/chapters/09-failure-modes-of-agentic-work.md
- **premise:** The chapter's opening is a perfect cold case: a clean six-bullet
  executive brief that silently omitted three dissenting documents in a subfolder
  the agent never reached. "Plausible output, incomplete action, confident
  delivery." Eight named failure modes, each with a surface sign.
- **thesis line:** "The failure you'll miss has no error message."
- **cold open:** `the brief looked perfect — what did it miss?`
- **verdict page:** "The surface signs — one page" (pick the 4–5 most
  recognizable of the chapter's eight failure modes).
- **note:** pairs with the existing when-cowork-helps reel (the stall) — this
  is the taxonomy that story was one instance of.

## E17 — Claude, Connected.
- **slug:** `claude-connected` · Wagwan: 4 → hello: **"Ahoj, Bear"** (Czech)
