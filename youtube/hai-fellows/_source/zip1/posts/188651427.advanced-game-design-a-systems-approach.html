Core Thesis

Game design is system design. Systems thinking is to the 21st century what literacy was to the 20th. Games provide the best experiential laboratory for learning to think systemically.

Three-Part Hypothesis:

  1. Game design and systems thinking are mutual lenses — each deepens understanding of the other

  2. Systemic game design theory grounds ad hoc practice in transferable principles

  3. Systems literacy is a critical 21st-century skill

PART I — FOUNDATIONS

Theory: What are systems? What are games? What is interactivity?

Chapter 1: Foundations of Systems

  • Ways of seeing: phenomenological → reductionist → holistic → systemic

  • Michigan Fish Test: cultural differences in part vs. whole perception

  • Reductionism (Descartes/Newton): useful but insufficient; leads to “cobra effect” unintended consequences

  • Holism: valuable but prone to false correlations

  • Systemic thinking: “both-and” — holds parts AND whole simultaneously

  • History of systems thinking: Bertalanffy, Wiener (cybernetics), Forrester (system dynamics), Holland, Meadows, Capra, Maturana/Varela, Luhmann

  • Key metaphors: wolves in Yellowstone (trophic cascade), rabbits in Australia, double pendulum, fireflies

  • Core insight: Systems must be experienced to be understood — games are the best vehicle for this

Chapter 2: Defining Systems

Formal definition: A set of parts that together form loops of interaction to create a persistent “whole” with properties not found in any single part.

Parts:

  • State (attributes + values), Boundaries, Behaviors

  • Types: Sources, Stocks, Sinks, Converters, Deciders

Simple → Complicated → Complex:

  • Complex = feedback loops present

  • Complicated = linear/sequential only

Loop Types:

  • Reinforcing (positive feedback): “rich get richer,” snowball effects

  • Balancing (negative feedback): equilibrium-seeking, stabilizing

Key concepts:

  • Nonlinearity, chaos vs. randomness

  • Lotka-Volterra predator-prey equations

  • Emergence, metastability, synergy

  • Upward & downward causality

  • Hierarchy and levels of organization

  • Structural coupling (horse+rider, game+player)

  • Depth (systems within systems) and Elegance (few rules, self-similar hierarchy, no exceptions)

Chapter 3: Foundations of Games and Game Design

Defining “game” (Huizinga, Caillois, Crawford, Meier, Costikyan, McGonigal, Salen/Zimmerman):

  • Magic circle, voluntary/nonconsequential, rules, interactivity, goals, feedback, outcomes

Frameworks: MDA (Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics), FBS/SBF

Systemic Model of Games — three levels:

LevelElementsParts (Structure)Tokens (nouns) + Rules (verbs) = Game MechanicsLoops (Function)Internal model, second-order design, player mental model, uncertainty, meaningful decisions, opposition, goalsWhole (Architecture/Theme)Content vs. systems balance, narrative, autotelic experience, meaning

Content-driven vs. Systemic games:

  • Content = linear, expensive, low replayability

  • Systemic = emergent, extensible, high replayability (Dwarf Fortress cat example)

Evolution of game design: apprenticeship → explicit theory (still forming)

PART II — PRINCIPLES

How to design systemic games

Chapter 5: Working as a Systemic Game Designer

Designer typologies:

  • Storytellers (start with whole) — risk: forgetting systems

  • Inventors (start with loops) — risk: forgetting the player

  • Toymakers (start with parts) — risk: forgetting goals/experience

Design process: iterate between parts ↔ loops ↔ whole (non-linear order)

Qualities of effective game systems (Achterman): Comprehensible, Consistent, Predictable, Extensible, Elegant

Tabletop vs. digital: tabletop forces rigor; “theatre makes you good”

Core practice: prototype fast, playtest early, iterate often (the designer’s loop)

Chapter 6: Designing the Whole Experience

Blue-sky design: brainstorming → twist ideas → “yes, and…” → curate

  • Constraints are your friend

  • Get past stereotypes/clichés

  • Diverse voices produce better ideas

The Concept Document (living document, tip of the iceberg):

Section 1 — High-Level Concept:

  • Working title

  • Concept statement (tweet-length)

  • “One Question” test (Does it rock? Is it authentic?)

  • Genre(s)

  • Target audience (psychographics/Quantic Foundry motivations, demographics, environment)

  • Unique Selling Points (USPs) / X-statements

Section 2 — Product Description:

  • Player experience (fantasy, key moments, emotions & meaning)

  • Visual & audio style

  • Game world fiction

  • Monetization (premium, F2P, DLC, ad-supported)

  • Technology, tools, platforms

  • Scope

Section 3 — Detailed Design:

  • Core loops

  • Objectives & progression

  • Narrative & main systems

  • Interactivity design

Elegance, Depth, Breadth: the three dimensions of game design richness; elegance is hardest to achieve

Chapter 7: Creating Game Loops

Review: reinforcing vs. balancing; loop components (sources, stocks, converters, deciders, sinks, resources, currencies)

The Four Principal Loops:

  1. Game’s model loop — the dynamic world the player inhabits

  2. Player’s mental loop — their growing understanding of the game

  3. Interactive loop — the core give-and-take (includes core loops)

  4. Designer’s loop — the outer balancing loop of design → playtest → adjust

Core loops: primary player activities at any given time; inner (fast) and outer (slow); stacking creates rhythm and engagement cycles

Three Kinds of Gameplay Loops:

TypeLoop CharacterResource BehaviorExamplesEnginesReinforcing OR balancingSame resourceMonopoly, Dominion, Splendor, RTS buildingEconomiesReinforcingExchange resourcesMost RPGs, trading games, MMO faucet/drainEcologiesBalancingExchange resourcesPredator/prey, environmental systems, Yellowstone wolves

Engine types: Boosting (invest vs. act trade-off) and Braking (limits/costs)

Economy issues: inflation (faucet/drain problem), tragedy of the commons, limits to growth, fixes that fail (cobra effect loops)

Ecology types: trophic cascades, arms races, symbiosis

Tools: Machinations diagramming, causal loop diagrams, system maps

Documenting systems: diagram + narrative description + links to detail docs
