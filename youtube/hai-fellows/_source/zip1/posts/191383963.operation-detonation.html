01 · VISION SUMMARY

“Operation Detonation is a cooperative squad shooter for PC and console, where victory is earned not by eliminating enemies but by engineering the most spectacular, structurally decisive environmental destruction possible — and surviving the chaos you created.”

Concept Formula

This game is a squad-based cooperative shooter for players who want to feel like architects of beautiful destruction, delivering the fantasy of controlled chaos through a physics-driven destruction system built on procedurally fractured environments. It occupies the space between Helldivers 2’s mission-driven cooperative intensity and Just Cause 4’s open sandbox physics spectacle, and it succeeds if the player feels like the most dangerous engineer in a world made of breakable things.

Target Player

One specific player: a 25–35 year old who has 800 hours in Helldivers 2, who clips every accidental chain-explosion, who instinctively looks at a building and thinks ‘I wonder if I could bring that down.’ They are frustrated by games where destruction is cosmetic — where a building absorbs a rocket and just plays a smoke animation. They want consequence. They want the ceiling to actually fall.

Platform & Scale

⚠ TIMELINE NOTE

The 36-month timeline assumes procedural destruction pipelines (Houdini-driven, per The Finals model) are validated in a 3-month pre-production prototype phase. If bespoke physics systems are required, timeline extends to 42 months. This decision must be locked by Week 8 of production.

02 · DESIGN PILLARS

These four pillars are non-negotiable experiential promises. Every feature must map to at least one. Any mechanic that serves none of them does not belong in this game.

PILLAR 1 · Earned Spectacle

The player should feel that every major explosion was the product of their deliberate engineering — not random luck or scripted spectacle.

HONORS: A structural integrity system where targeted demolition of load-bearing columns produces asymmetric, unpredictable collapse. The player who studied the building wins.

VIOLATES: Pre-scripted cinematic destruction events that play regardless of player positioning or method. Spectacle the player didn’t cause is a movie, not a game.

FAILURE STATE: If the physics budget is cut and destruction is replaced with mesh-swapping (smoke + rubble pile), the pillar collapses. Players will detect the trick within minutes.

PILLAR 2 · Consequential Physics

The world must react honestly to what the player does. Destruction creates hazards, reshapes the battlefield, and changes what is possible.

HONORS: Collapsed walls create new cover. A destroyed bridge changes enemy patrol routes. Crater terrain slows specific enemy types.

VIOLATES: Any environmental feature that cannot be damaged at all. Invincible pillars or indestructible floor plates are design lies that break trust.

FAILURE STATE: A world that looks destructible but snaps back to original state after 30 seconds. Impermanence without consequence is aesthetic, not design.

PILLAR 3 · Controlled Danger

The tools for destruction are the same tools that can kill the player. This tension — between power and self-preservation — is the game’s core emotional engine.

HONORS: Explosive radius that does not discriminate between enemy and allied units. A misplaced 500kg drop kills teammates. This is not a bug to be patched — it is the feature.

VIOLATES: Friendly-fire immunity options in standard play. ‘Safe zones’ around allied units that reduce explosive radius. These remove the tension that gives power its meaning.

FAILURE STATE: When damage numbers become optimizable and the game becomes a spreadsheet. The player should be afraid of their own toolkit.

PILLAR 4 · Visual Honesty

The visual footprint of every effect must accurately communicate the mechanical footprint. Player trust is built or broken here.

HONORS: Explosive radius markers that visually match the blast zone. Smoke clouds that truthfully represent the obscured area. Damage numbers that correlate to visual impact.

VIOLATES: A mushroom cloud that looks world-ending but has a 5-meter kill radius. Players will not forgive this. See: Helldivers 2 500kg patch history.

FAILURE STATE: A marketing trailer that shows destruction at maximum fidelity settings that no target hardware can sustain. Ship what you can run.

Pillar Collision Test

PILLAR 1 (Earned Spectacle) vs. PILLAR 3 (Controlled Danger): These are in structural tension. Spectacle rewards scale; danger punishes it. Resolution: Controlled Danger is PRIMARY in direct conflict. The game does not reduce self-damage to let the player achieve bigger explosions safely. The player earns spectacle by mastering risk management, not by removing risk.

PILLAR 2 (Consequential Physics) vs. timeline: Full physics simulation at the scale this pillar demands is a Top 2 production risk (see Section 14 Risk Register). The physics budget is protected. Visual fidelity in other areas is sacrificed first.

03 · CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

Micro Loop · 30 Seconds

The moment-to-moment unit of play.

PLAYER DECISION: Which target, which tool, and where to stand.

RISK OF FAILURE: Self-kill or team wipe from miscalculated radius.

REWARD FOR SUCCESS: Structural cascade — the intended target falls and takes secondary objectives with it.

Meso Loop · 5–15 Minutes

The operational unit of play — one mission segment.

  • Squad inserts into an objective zone with a fixed loadout of explosive assets.

  • Objective type determines the ‘puzzle’: eliminate fortified position, destroy supply infrastructure, or hold a perimeter while structures collapse around you.

  • Resource management: each explosive tool has a use count. Mid-mission resupply is available but exposed — you trade safety for replenishment.

  • Mission ends with a Performance Debrief: Destruction Efficiency rating (did you hit the right targets?), Friendly Fire Incidents, Most Expensive Mistake.

PLAYER DECISION: Loadout optimization for the objective type. When to resupply vs. press advantage.

RISK: Running dry on CORE assets mid-objective, forcing improvisation with secondary tools.

REWARD: Structural Cascade bonus — if secondary destruction was triggered by primary action, multiplier applied.

Macro Loop · Session to Session

  • Unlockable explosive archetypes: new tools with different physics profiles (upward cone vs. spherical vs. shaped charge).

  • Persistent Destruction Rating — a cumulative cross-session score that unlocks cosmetic and modifier content.

  • Dynamic mission difficulty scaling based on enemy adaptation: if the player destroys cover consistently, enemies begin deploying mobile, indestructible bunkers. The game learns the player’s method.

WHAT BRINGS THE PLAYER BACK: An unlocked tool they haven’t stress-tested. A new map with architecture they want to take apart.

UNFINISHED QUESTION: Did the squad’s approach get adapted against? What changed?

LOOP HONESTY TEST

Strip this loop of all visual fidelity, sound design, and setting. Replace explosions with ‘block removed.’ The decision of WHERE to remove a block — and surviving the structural consequence — is satisfying as an abstraction. The game does not require its visuals to have a core loop. This passes.

05 · CORE MECHANICS

MECHANIC A · Structural Integrity System

THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES

Without load-bearing logic, destruction is cosmetic. Players cannot engineer a collapse — they can only trigger pre-scripted animations. This system makes the player a structural engineer, not a button-presser.

HOW IT WORKS

  • Every destructible building is composed of Blocks and Links. Blocks are rigid geometry units. Links are constraint connections (wood, concrete, steel — each with a defined Break Threshold in kN).

  • When a Block is removed or its integrity falls below 50%, all Links attached to it are stress-evaluated. If the transferred load exceeds a connected Block’s rated capacity, that Block fails.

  • Failure propagates until the structure reaches a new stable state or full collapse.

  • The system runs on the CPU physics thread. Per-frame budget: 12ms on PS5/Series X. Overflow triggers LOD reduction on distant destruction events — never cancellation.

PILLAR ALIGNMENT

PILLAR 1 (Earned Spectacle): A player who identifies and targets load-bearing blocks produces cascade collapses unavailable to players who fire randomly.

PILLAR 2 (Consequential Physics): Collapse creates new geometry that persists, alters cover, and changes pathing.

LOOP PLACEMENT

MICRO LOOP — Step 1 (Assess) and Step 3 (React). The system is the read and the consequence.

EDGE CASES

  • EDGE CASE 1: Player removes all ground-floor blocks. Expected: full gravity collapse, all floors descend in sequence. Failure if unhandled: physics solver attempts simultaneous resolution of 500+ block-link recalculations, causing frame spike. MITIGATION: Chunk-phase the collapse — floor 1 resolves before floor 2 begins calculating. Priority: CORE.

  • EDGE CASE 2: Enemy unit is standing on a collapsing floor. Expected: unit becomes an active physics body mid-animation, falls with debris, takes fall damage. Failure if unhandled: enemy clips through falling geometry, teleports to ground, resumes patrol. Priority: CORE.

  • EDGE CASE 3: Player attempts to stand on partially collapsed floor with >60% integrity removed. Expected: floor sags, then gives, with a 1.5s warning crack audio cue before failure. Failure if unhandled: player is phased through floor polygon, falls without feedback — feels like a bug, not physics. Priority: CORE.

SCOPE BOUNDARY

This system governs pre-built destructible architecture ONLY. Terrain deformation (craters) is a separate system (/s2, Crater Physics). Natural geography (hills, rock formations) is PERMANENTLY INDESTRUCTIBLE in this release.

  • EDGE CASE 1: Two charges detonate simultaneously, overlapping radii. Expected: radii are additive, not averaged. Structural damage calculated from both blast centers independently, then summed. Priority: CORE.

  • EDGE CASE 2: Player deploys charge inside a collapsing structure that then falls on the charge before detonation. Expected: charge detonates at original position with full radius, plus triggers secondary debris shrapnel from the fallen material above it. Priority: IMPORTANT.

  • EDGE CASE 3: Player uses Column Cutter on a building that has already had columns removed on floors above. Expected: structural recalculation runs from the removed column upward, not downward — collapse propagates to already-weakened upper floors first. Priority: CORE.

06 · SYSTEMS DOCUMENTATION

SYSTEM A · VFX Hierarchy and Particle Budget

Design Reason

An explosion in this game is not one effect — it is a precisely timed hierarchy of emitters. Collapse this into a single asset and the explosion loses weight, read, and emotional impact. The hierarchy is also the optimization layer.

Component Stack (execution order, millisecond offsets)

PERFORMANCE RULE

If frame time exceeds 14ms during an explosion, Layers 5 and 6 are the first to reduce fidelity. Layers 1–3 are NEVER culled — they carry the mechanical read. A player must always be able to see the blast core and shockwave to judge whether they are inside the kill radius.

SYSTEM B · Procedural Destruction Pipeline (Houdini)

Design Reason

Manual fracturing of every destructible structure is economically unsustainable at the content volume this game requires. The Finals shipped 100+ destructible buildings with a team of 10–20, using a Houdini-driven procedural pipeline. This game adopts the same model.

Pipeline Stages

  • STAGE 1 (Architect): Level designers block out building footprint and material designations (wood, concrete, steel, glass) in Unreal.

  • STAGE 2 (Generate): Houdini Building Creator script reads blockout, applies material-specific fracture patterns, generates fractured mesh, collision hulls, and structural link data automatically. Time per building: 4–6 minutes.

  • STAGE 3 (Tag): Technical artist reviews generated output, manually tags a maximum of 5 ‘hero’ collapse moments per building for cinematic camera hints. No manual fracture adjustment allowed — if the output requires manual adjustment, the input blockout is wrong.

  • STAGE 4 (Validate): QA runs automated structural integrity simulation — every building must survive a minimum of 3 test demolitions without physics solver overflow.

Player Legibility

The structural system is PARTIALLY VISIBLE to the player. Material type is communicated through visual surface treatment (wood = visible grain and charring, concrete = crack propagation preview). The precise load-bearing calculation is HIDDEN — the player learns it through experience, not a UI meter.

SYSTEM C · Destruction Network Replication

Design Reason

In a 4-player cooperative session, every player must see the same building collapse in the same way at the same time. Competitive fairness and cooperative coordination both depend on this. Replication is not a post-ship concern — it is a core architecture decision that must be resolved in pre-production.

Replication Model

  • Server-authoritative physics: the host simulation is canonical. All clients receive destruction state deltas, not re-simulated local physics.

  • Structural state is serialized as a Block Integrity Array — each block’s health percentage, transmitted at 20Hz during active destruction events, 2Hz at rest.

  • Client-side prediction is used only for visual effects (particles, debris visuals). Structural state changes are never predicted client-side — a desync in structural state is a game-breaking competitive bug.

CRITICAL DEPENDENCY: The Cascade Charge mechanic (Tier 3 explosive) requires sequential detonation events to be server-timed to within 50ms. Latency above 150ms to host will produce visible desyncs. Design constraint: Cascade Charge is disabled in cross-region matchmaking by default.

07 · PLAYER PROGRESSION ARCHITECTURE

Three Curves

All three curves must be designed together. A skill curve without a matching challenge curve produces boredom. A resource curve without a skill curve produces pay-to-win.

Difficulty Spike Justification

Intended Flow State: Hours 3–7 (Early Game, T1 mastery). Players are competent at reading single structures and managing one or two tools. Everything works.

SPIKE — Hour 8 (Multi-floor concrete introduction): Concrete structures require 3× the T1 charges to demolish the same footprint as wood. Players who used wood strategies on concrete run dry. This spike is intentional — it forces loadout reconsideration and introduces the T2 Concussive Mortar as a pre-weakening tool, not a replacement for T1 precision.

Anti-Patterns Addressed

GRIND TRAP SAFEGUARD: Resource unlocks are gated by Mission Type Completion (first successful steel-structure demolition unlocks Column Cutter), not by playtime or currency grinding. A player cannot unlock T3 tools by farming T1 missions.

POWER CLIFF SAFEGUARD: T3 Strategic Ordnance has EXTREME self-risk. A player who switches from T2 to T3 without mastering positioning will die more, not less. The power increase is real; so is the skill requirement to survive it.

11 · RISK REGISTER

Top 3 Risks Summary

RISK 1 — Physics Budget Overflow: The structural system and VFX hierarchy are designed to run simultaneously at peak load — four players triggering Tier 3 ordnance inside a dense multi-story concrete structure. On PS5, the combined physics and particle budget leaves 5ms of headroom against target frame time. A single optimization miss at content production will push this over. This risk is not hypothetical — Arrowhead documented client crashes from particle overload in Helldivers 2. Mitigation requires a combined load test prototype by Month 6, not Month 18.

RISK 2 — Institutional Knowledge Loss: The structural integrity system is complex, bespoke, and built on a specific understanding of UE5 Chaos behavior. Helldivers 2’s technical struggles with the Stingray engine post-Autodesk deprecation are a documented precedent for what happens when institutional knowledge is unavailable. This studio cannot afford that scenario. Pair programming on structural systems and mandatory documentation are not optional — they are existential risk management.

RISK 3 — Visual/Mechanical Radius Mismatch: This is the most player-visible failure mode in the entire design. The Helldivers 2 500kg bomb required a mid-season patch to increase explosion radius because players’ visual expectations from the mushroom cloud were not met by the mechanical radius. That patch required regression testing across every enemy type and environmental object in the game. Ship with alignment baked in — do not plan to patch this.
