Based on Archaeological Evidence from Team Cherry’s Development Artifacts

This document is a reconstruction, not a transcript. Every claim is derived from documented sources: the Ludum Dare 27 submission, the 2014 Kickstarter campaign and its stretch goal structure, Ari Gibson’s public interviews, the PlayMaker FSM architecture visible in the Unity build, and the Backer Character Creation Document. Where evidence is incomplete, the document is marked [INFERRED] and the reasoning is shown. Nothing here was invented.

1. THE VISION

One-Sentence Pitch: A knight explores the ruins of a bug civilization and chooses whether to end it or become it.

That’s the game. Everything else is detail.

Design Pillars

Pillar 1: The World Exists Without You. Hallownest doesn’t care that you arrived. The Crossroads were already infected. Greenpath was already growing over the old roads. The City of Tears was already flooding. The knight enters a world in motion. Our job is to make that world legible through exploration, not exposition. No narrator explains what happened. The lore tablets don’t address the player. The bugs you meet are continuing lives they were living before you got here.

Pillar 2: Cute and Spook Must Coexist. This is a non-negotiable aesthetic constraint. The Crawlid is adorable. The Failed Champion is terrifying. Both are true simultaneously. If a design tips entirely into horror, we’ve lost the game. If it tips entirely into cuteness, we’ve lost the stakes. The Backer Character Creation Document formalized this balance as “Heart, Soul, and Stamina” — rounded shapes for accessibility, elongated limbs for hostility, mask-like faces for isolation. These aren’t vibes. They’re rules.

Pillar 3: Movement Is Mastery. The knight’s movement vocabulary is deliberately small at the start: walk, jump, nail swing, fall. The skill ceiling exists in what players discover they can do with those inputs — the buffered jump off a slanted wall, the pogo on an enemy’s head, the nail bounce through a hazard floor. We don’t document these. The world teaches them by requiring them. If a secret requires a technique, the geometry surrounding it will suggest that technique to a player paying attention.

Pillar 4: Difficulty Curves Are Optional. The critical path through Hallownest is completable by a patient player with no particular mechanical skill. Optional content — the White Palace, the Colosseum, the Radiance — is tuned for mastery. We originally intended more extreme difficulty throughout, but the data from playtesting showed players quitting mid-experience. The hard stuff moves to the edges. The main path stays honest.

Non-Goals

We are not making a Metroidvania. This is not a rejection of the genre — it’s a rejection of the label as a design tool. If we think “Metroidvania,” we make a Metroidvania. If we think “a ruined civilization underground,” we make Hallownest. Genre conventions are fine when they serve the world. They get cut when they don’t.

We are not making a story players are told. No cutscenes that explain the Pale King’s decision. No narrator who contextualizes the Infection. The story lives in item descriptions, NPC fragments, and what the player sees when they Dream Nail a boss’s corpse. The player’s reconstruction is the story.

We are not managing a task backlog. [This is documented. Gibson asked “What is Jira?” in a 2025 interview. The studio operated on a shared monthly plan and conversation.] We’re building a world month by month. If a character makes both directors laugh in conversation, it goes in. The documentation of that decision is the character existing in the game.

We are not including a “See Breakable Walls” charm. It was designed. It was cut. The environment is the document. If a wall is breakable, the geometry will feel slightly wrong to a player who’s paying attention. We trust the player to pay attention.

Player Fantasy

You are small. The world is vast and ruined and beautiful and hostile. You are moving through the remains of something that was once great, and the greatness is still visible in the architecture even as the architecture crumbles. You don’t know yet what your connection to this place is. When you find out, it recontextualizes everything you’ve already seen.

The fantasy isn’t power. It’s comprehension.

2. CORE LOOP & MECHANICS

The Loop: Explore → Encounter → Die or Succeed → Gain resource or ability → Explore further

That’s it. The loop doesn’t change from the Forgotten Crossroads to the White Palace. What changes is the player.

The 3Cs

Character: The knight moves with “snappy, 2-jointed spring legs” — this is documented in Gibson’s description of the movement philosophy. Acceleration is immediate. There is no slide, no momentum tax. The knight goes where you point it. This is a deliberate design choice: environmental difficulty comes from what the world places in front of the player, not from the character fighting the player’s inputs.

Jump height: approximately 3.5 character heights. Nail reach: approximately 1 character width. These are not verified exact numbers — the FSM implementation defines them — but they’re proportional to the visual design. [INFERRED from sprite proportions and observable gameplay.]

Camera: Side-scrolling, locked to a room until transition triggers. Camera follows the knight at a slight lag. This lag is intentional — it gives players a moment of visual advance warning in the direction of travel. The camera doesn’t pan aggressively; it settles.

Controls: 4-directional movement. Jump (buffers for a few frames on ledge edge — the “coyote time” is not documented explicitly but is observable and consistent with the snappy movement philosophy). Nail swing in 4 directions. Spell casting draws from Soul. Dash requires the Mothwing Cloak — not available at start.

Soul and the Focus System

The Soul system originated as the “Juice Timer” in Hungry Knight (Ludum Dare 27, 2013). In that version, a countdown forced players to kill enemies to replenish energy or die. The documentation for the transition from Hungry Knight to Hollow Knight shows a deliberate philosophical shift: from survival-based resource (you die without it) to choice-based resource (you heal with it, or you cast spells with it, but you choose which).

The mechanic: Every nail hit on an enemy fills the Soul vessel. A full vessel equals 33 Soul. Focus costs 33 Soul and heals one Mask. Spell casting costs 33 Soul. The player chooses between offense and healing with every vessel filled.

This is the game’s central tension in a resource loop. Every fight asks: are you ahead enough to spend Soul on spells, or do you need it for healing?

What the Soul system is not: A mana pool. Mana refills over time. Soul requires aggression. A player who retreats and waits never fills their vessel. The mechanic punishes passivity. This is documented in the design intent and legible in the implementation.

The Nail

The Nail is a sword. It behaves like a sword. It swings in an arc, it has reach, it can be upgraded four times through Pale Ore at the Nailsmith in the City of Tears.

The Nail is not called a sword. It’s called a Nail. This is documentation of the world, not the mechanic — in Hallownest, bugs don’t carry swords. They carry nails.

Nail upgrades are linear. They do not branch. This is documented in the Kickstarter stretch goals, which added the Dream Nail as a separate system rather than complicating the primary weapon.

The Dream Nail: A separate weapon that reads the residual emotion of enemies, objects, and corpses. It deals negligible damage. Its function is narrative — it lets the player see the last thoughts of anything in Hallownest. The documentation of Hallownest’s history is accessed through this mechanic. The lore IS the weapon’s function.

Charms

Charms are equippable modifiers. They cost Notches. The player starts with 3 Notches and can acquire more.

The Charm system exists to solve a design problem: a single-weapon character in a Metroidvania-style world risks feeling limited mid-game. Charms extend the player’s build identity without branching the core movement or combat systems.

Design constraint: No charm should make the game trivially easy. Quick Slash is the closest to overpowered; it increases attack speed significantly. The balance is that it costs 3 Notches — a substantial investment.

Documented early Charms that were cut or modified:

  • “Money Magnet” → became Gathering Swarm (documented in the early Game Informer Map)

  • “White Crystal Ring” (chargeable Vengeful Spirit) → cut entirely

  • “See Breakable Walls” → cut in favor of environmental design

The cut list is documentation of the design philosophy becoming more confident. Early Team Cherry wanted to help players. Later Team Cherry trusted them.

Death and Shade

On death, the player drops their Geo (currency) at the death location as a Shade. The Shade blocks the path. The player must return, kill it, and recover their Geo.

The Shade is not just a penalty mechanic. It is a hostile version of the player character. You killed yourself, and now you must defeat that death to recover from it. This is documented nowhere explicitly — it’s a design statement that lives in the mechanic.

The Joni’s Blessing charm replaces the standard Mask system with a single large Mask and no Shade on death. It’s a documented accessibility option that Team Cherry included without framing it as difficulty assistance.

3. NARRATIVE & WORLD

The Lore Is Not the Story

The story of Hollow Knight is: the Knight descends into Hallownest, defeats the three Dreamers, enters the Black Egg Temple, and either destroys or becomes the Hollow Knight, or ascends to fight the Radiance. This takes approximately 25 hours for a completionist run.

That is not the story Team Cherry was documenting.

The story Team Cherry was documenting is: a god of light was forgotten. A Pale King tried to contain her infection by creating a vessel. The vessel failed. The infection spread. Everything you see is the aftermath. The Knight may be another attempt at a vessel, or the Knight may be the one who finally ends it.

That second story is never stated. It is assembled by the player from lore tablets, NPC fragments, item descriptions, and Dream Nail readings. The “Actual Story Document” at Team Cherry was not a script. It was a collection of flavor text, a shared understanding of what Hallownest was, and the question: what would you find here?

World Structure

Hallownest is underground. It is organized vertically: the Forgotten Crossroads at the top, the Ancient Basin and the Abyss at the bottom. The City of Tears sits in the middle, accessible from multiple directions, serving as the game’s hub-adjacent zone once unlocked.

Documented Design Intent vs. Implementation:

The Boneforest cut is the clearest documentation of Team Cherry’s editorial philosophy. The art existed. The space would have filled the map. It was removed because it didn’t satisfy the theme as well as the Abyss and Ancient Basin. The documentation was subservient to the feeling of the world. A bigger map was not a better map.

Characters

The Knight: No dialogue. No confirmed backstory until the Abyss sequence. The emptiness is the character. [This is documented in the design intent: vessels must be hollow to contain the Infection.]

Hornet: Aggressive, fast, appears as a recurring boss. Her backstory connects to the Pale King and Queen Vespa. The documentation of her “wider range of movement” and “lithe height” was the reason she couldn’t fit into Hallownest for Silksong — she physically broke the world geometry. That incompatibility became a sequel.

Quirrel: An amnesiac explorer who follows the Knight’s path. He is present at most major discoveries. He disappears after the Lake of Unn. His final scene — sitting at the Blue Lake, then gone — is documented nowhere in any design artifact. It’s the world suggesting what happens to adventurers who finish their quest.

The Pale King: Never fought. Seen once in a flashback. His absence is his characterization. He made a terrible decision with the best possible intentions and it destroyed everything. The documentation of the Pale King is the architecture of Hallownest — the roads he built, the seals he placed, the throne room that’s now empty.

Tone

The Wanderer’s Journal (written from Ellina the Chronicler’s perspective) documents the world as someone who was present before the infection. It provides creature descriptions and area notes without major revelations. Its function is: this world was real before you got here. The documentation of that reality is a bug’s first-person account.

The world is not dark. It is elegiac. There’s a difference. Dark worlds are hostile. Elegiac worlds are grieving. Hallownest is grieving.

4. LEVEL DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Team Cherry did not design levels first. They built spaces that felt like ruins and then made the platforming fit. This is documented explicitly by Gibson.

The design question for every area: What would you expect to find here? Where could that lead? Who might you find there?

Forgotten Crossroads: The entry point. Wide corridors. Forgiving geometry. The enemies are simple — Crawlids, Vengeflies, Gruzlings. The documentation of this area is pedagogical: by the time the player reaches Hornet, they have learned to jump, to swing their Nail, to read enemy patterns. They haven’t been told any of this. The Crossroads taught it through consequence.

The late-game “Infected Crossroads” transformation is documented in the stretch goal structure and the thematic arc. The area the player started in is unrecognizable by the end. The transformation forces backtracking. The documentation of that backtrack is the player remembering what it looked like before.

White Palace: Documented as a “trap-filled Dusk Knight gauntlet” in early design. Bosses were planned and cut. What shipped is pure platforming — saw blades, path-of-pain geometry, precise jumps over void. Ari Gibson built this alone in a matter of days. The documentation for the White Palace is Gibson’s sketchbook + whatever he was thinking during those days. It is the game’s hardest optional content. It is also completely unnecessary to finish the game. That’s the design: difficulty for players who want it, invisible to players who don’t.

The Abyss: The bottom of the world. The documentation of this area is the Knight’s origin — the Birthplace sequence shows where vessels like the Knight were created and discarded. The cut “Abyss Expansion” (four additional bosses, journey into the twisted space within the lake of void) was never funded. What remains is enough: the shore, the darkness, the answer to what the Knight is.

5. ENEMY AI DOCUMENTATION (FSM ARCHITECTURE)

Team Cherry did not write Technical Design Documents for enemy behavior. They built PlayMaker Finite State Machines in Unity. The FSM is the document.

Reconstructed: Aspid Hunter FSM (internally: “Spitter-spitter”)

The “Infection Bullet” variable name is documentation of the world inside the code. The Aspid isn’t firing a projectile. It’s spreading infection. That’s in the variable name.

Boss Complexity: Hornet Protector’s FSM manages dashes, jumps, thread attacks, and evasive repositioning through dozens of states. The Radiance manages phase transitions, attack pattern escalation, and the final “ascension” sequence. These are not documented in any design brief. They exist in the FSM graphs. A developer debugging the Radiance fight opens Unity and reads the graph.

Difficulty documentation: Gibson noted they originally intended more extreme difficulty segments throughout the game but cut them after playtesting showed players abandoning the experience. The FSM documentation of difficulty is the tuning of “Fire Timer” variables and transition conditions. When a boss feels too aggressive, a developer changes a float. The design intent is implicit in the numbers.

6. TECHNICAL CONSTRAINTS

Engine: Unity. This is documented and not in dispute.

Visual scripting: PlayMaker for all enemy AI and boss logic. This decision was not a concession to the team’s technical limitations — it was a workflow choice that allowed Ari Gibson and William Pellen to collaborate on behavior without requiring a traditional programmer for every enemy. The FSM viewer is legible to non-programmers. That’s the point.

Asset pipeline: Sketch → phone photograph → digital cleanup → Unity import. The art bible is the sketchbook. This is documented in Gibson’s interviews.

Platform target: PC first. The Wii U stretch goal ($50,000 AUD) was reached but later pivoted to Nintendo Switch when Nintendo announced the platform. This is documented in the Kickstarter update history. Console porting was managed by Jack Vine.

What they didn’t support: Traditional project management software. External QA teams. Published changelogs during development. These are not oversights. They’re choices that kept the team moving.

7. ECONOMY

Currency is Geo. Enemies drop it. The player carries it until they die.

Resource sinks: Relics (sold to Lemm), Charms (bought from Salubra, Sly, Iselda), map upgrades (Cornifer’s maps, Iselda’s pins), Vessel Fragments, Mask Shards, Nail upgrades.

Resource sources: Enemy drops (linear, consistent), Geo deposits (one-time, hidden), Grub rewards (from Grubfather).

The economy is not documented as a system. It emerged from the world. Geo is what bugs use. The blacksmith takes Pale Ore, which is rarer than Geo and found in the world rather than dropped by enemies. Pale Ore is therefore more valuable than Geo not because a design document said so, but because the world treats it that way.

Monetization: Hollow Knight launched at $14.99 USD. It is a premium game. Four DLC packs (Hidden Dreams, The Grimm Troupe, Lifeblood, Godmaster) were released free to all owners. This is documented in the post-launch Kickstarter updates. The decision to release paid content free was not economically obvious — it was a statement about what the game owed its players.

8. NON-GOALS & CUT CONTENT

Cut: Boneforest. A large area planned for the bottom of the map. Art existed. Cut because it didn’t satisfy the theme as well as the Abyss and Ancient Basin. The documentation of this cut is Gibson’s statement that the world suggests what should exist — and the Boneforest didn’t fit what the world was suggesting.

Cut: White Palace Bosses. The “Dusk Knight” gauntlet was intended to include boss encounters. Cut in favor of pure platforming. The remaining challenge (Path of Pain) is optional within the already-optional White Palace.

Cut: See Breakable Walls charm. Documented in the early Game Informer Map. Cut when the team decided the environment was the document. Breakable walls are breakable because they look slightly wrong. That’s sufficient.

Cut: White Crystal Ring (chargeable Vengeful Spirit). Mechanical complication of the spell system that didn’t survive the editorial process.

Transferred: 2nd Playable Character (Hornet). The $56,000 AUD stretch goal committed to a second playable character. Hornet was designed, prototyped, and found to be incompatible with Hallownest’s world geometry. She became Silksong. The documentation of this decision is: when your character breaks the world you built, you build her a new world.

Not-yet-built at launch: Abyss Expansion. The $85,000 AUD stretch goal was not met. The journey into the twisted space within the lake of void, with four additional bosses, was reduced to the Abyss Shore and the Birthplace sequence. This is documented in the campaign goal history. The content that shipped is not a failure of the expanded vision — it’s a more concentrated version of the same thematic intent.

CLOSING NOTE

The design document for Hollow Knight is Hollow Knight.

The sketchbooks are the art bible. The FSM graphs are the technical bible. The Kickstarter stretch goals are the roadmap. The Backer Character Creation Document is the style guide. The Wanderer’s Journal is the lore compendium. The world itself — its geometry, its variable names, its cut content and its kept content — is the record of every decision Team Cherry made over two years.

What this reconstruction cannot capture is the conversation. Gibson and Pellen sat across from each other. A character made them laugh. It went in. A mechanic felt wrong. It came out. The documentation of those moments is the game shipping in February 2017.

The most effective design document is the world itself. Team Cherry proved it.
