The technical foundation is well-established. In 1999, Messod Beneish developed a mathematical formula—the M-Score—that retrospectively identified Enron’s accounting manipulations years before the company collapsed. Eight financial ratios calculate the probability that a company has inflated its reported health. Days’ Sales in Receivables (DSRI) catches channel stuffing. Asset Quality Index (AQI) flags when companies capitalize costs that should be expensed. Total Accruals to Total Assets (TATA)—the most powerful signal—measures the gap between accounting profits and actual cash.

A Sloan Ratio above 15% is a red flag. Above 25%, it’s a siren.
