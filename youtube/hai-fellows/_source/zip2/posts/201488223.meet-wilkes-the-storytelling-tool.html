If your organization has true stories worth telling but never quite enough time or talent to tell them well, this tool was built for you.

Who Is Paul Wilkes?

Wilkes is named for, and modeled on, the journalist Paul Wilkes, and his story is the reason the tool works the way it does.

Paul Wilkes is a journalist, author of more than twenty books, and documentarian whose work has appeared in The New Yorker, The Atlantic, and The New York Times Magazine. His 1970s documentary series, Six American Families, won a DuPont-Columbia award for telling a large human story by focusing intently on a few ordinary lives.

In 2006, on a trip to India, he encountered a street girl who had been deliberately blinded to make her a “better beggar.” That moment changed everything. He founded Homes of Hope India, which has since built dozens of safe homes for orphaned, abandoned, and trafficked girls. He still lives in Wilmington, North Carolina, and still writes.

What makes his journalism worth building a tool around is its restraint. Wilkes never lectures. He shows a real person in a real place, and trusts the reader to feel the weight of it. The Wilkes tool puts that craft — intimate, specific, witness-based, and authentic— into the hands of organizations that have important work to share but aren’t staffed with professional writers.

What Wilkes Does

Wilkes generates long-form narrative writing in that tradition. Instead of producing generic copy, it helps you build a story with a real arc and a human center. It can draft:

  • Long-form profiles - the story of one person, told closely

  • Documentary arcs - a sequence that follows change over time

  • Social-entrepreneur pieces - how a mission began and where it’s going

  • Literary reviews - thoughtful, narrative-driven commentary

  • YouTube packages - scripts and narration for video storytelling

  • Field-log narratives - on-the-ground notes turned into readable accounts

It’s part of the broader Humanitarians AI ecosystem, built around a simple idea we take seriously: teaching what AI cannot do, and documenting that learning honestly as it happens. Wilkes reflects that. It’s a drafting partner, not a replacement for the person who actually witnessed the story. You bring the truth; it helps you shape it.

How It Works in Practice

The workflow is simple, which is the point. You bring the raw material, what happened, who was there, what you saw, and Wilkes turns it into a first draft with structure, pacing, and a human focal point. From there, you edit, fact-check, and add the details only you know.

That’s exactly how we used it for the Homes of Hope Substack. We weren’t starting from a blank page anymore. We were starting from a draft that already understood how to tell the story, and we shaped it into something a stranger would read to the end.

Why Nonprofits Need This

Most nonprofits run on stretched time. The person who knows the stories is usually the same person doing ten other jobs, so good storytelling slides to the bottom of the list, and the result is a website full of statistics nobody remembers.

Wilkes lowers that barrier dramatically. It turns the founder, the field coordinator, or the volunteer who just got back into a storyteller, without requiring any of them to be writers. For a cause like Homes of Hope, where one girl’s story does more for understanding than any chart ever could, that shift is everything.

It also keeps the writing honest. Because the tool is built around the witness-based style, it pushes toward concrete detail instead of vague inspiration, the kind of writing donors trust, because it sounds like a person rather than a brochure.

Uses for Everyone

Wilkes was made for mission-driven work, and the use cases run across an entire organization:

For communicators and fundraisers. Turn field notes into newsletters, appeal letters, and grant narratives that lead with a person instead of a number. A strong opening profile can carry an entire annual report.

For founders and program staff. Capture the origin of your work and the moments that prove it’s working — your story stays yours; the tool just helps you tell it.

For volunteers and field workers. Convert a day’s messy notes into a clean field-log narrative the whole team, and the public, can follow.

For social entrepreneurs and partners. Document a mission’s growth in a way that earns attention from donors, press, and new collaborators.

For students and aspiring writers. Study narrative structure in action, how a profile is built, how detail earns emotion, how a writer shows instead of tells.
