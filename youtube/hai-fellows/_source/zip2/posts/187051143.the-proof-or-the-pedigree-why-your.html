The Mechanics: What Gets Verified

When you submit a repository, DevProof’s verification engine analyzes:

Authorship percentage: What percentage of the complex logic carries your commit signature? The threshold is 10%—if you contributed less than that to a project’s core functionality, it doesn’t qualify. This isn’t arbitrary. It’s the difference between “I built this” and “I contributed to this.”

Code complexity: Not lines of code. Cyclomatic complexity, nesting depth, abstraction patterns. The system distinguishes between someone who wrote a sophisticated state management solution and someone who pasted boilerplate from Stack Overflow.

Structural quality: Test coverage, documentation, modularity. Does this code look like it was built by someone who understands production systems, or does it look like a first draft that accidentally works?

Temporal coherence: Did this project evolve over time with iterative commits, or did massive blocks of complex code appear overnight? The latter suggests AI generation or plagiarism.

The output: A verification certificate with your authorship score, complexity index, and structural quality metrics. This certificate makes you searchable.
