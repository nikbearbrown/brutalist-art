A how-to ai-explainer series for **INFO 7375 Assignment 9 — Making Your Brand Unforgettable**
(a PDF + a published article, 100 pts, due July 24, 2026). Audience: **students who have a brand
and a tool but have never written a brand story or published an article.** Register: **pragmatic,
step-by-step** (Liam voice, `claude-liam`, Kokoro am_onyx). The series walks a beginner from "I
have accomplishments" to "three battle-ready mini-stories, one expanded About-page narrative, and
a live published article."

## The core insight (say it in E1 and hold it all series)

**The best brand stories aren't about you — they're about a transformation.** A problem
encountered, a decision made, a change that happened — and the reader recognizes something of
themselves in it. Every episode filters the writing through that: not "look what I did," but
"here's a change you'll recognize."

## Honesty / craft threads (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW) — this is a writing assignment, so voice IS the grade

- **Sound like a human, not a LinkedIn post generator.** The named failure mode. Use Claude to
  STRUCTURE raw bullets into an arc — but the specific details, the authentic voice, and the
  decision about what's actually true and meaningful are the student's. This is the same
  human-in-the-loop / +1 principle from the Madison touchpoints: the AI drafts, the human owns
  the truth.
- **Specific, concrete details — no generic statements.** A real moment, a real number, a real
  name beats "I'm passionate about innovation" every time.
- **The three arcs must be DISTINCT.** Different frameworks, different angles — "too similar" is a
  named pitfall.
- **Published LIVE, not just a PDF.** A real URL on LinkedIn Articles / Medium / Substack — a piece
  that only exists in the submission doesn't count.
- **Write it in your own voice — not the voice you think a brand should have.** (The expanded story
  brief says this outright.)

## Series doctrine (every episode)

- **Assume zero storytelling craft.** Define every term first time (narrative framework, arc,
  Hero's Journey, five-point structure, turning point, CTA, subhead, scannable). Never "just."
- **One deliverable per episode**, ending in the real written piece, with the paste-ready Claude
  prompt that STRUCTURES it from the student's raw bullets — then the "make it sound like you" pass.
- **Bring raw material, not a blank page.** Every episode starts by having the student paste
  bullets (key moments, outcomes) — Claude shapes; the student sharpens.
- **ai-explainer bookends stay:** composer cold open → step-by-step body → verdict recap → Your
  Turn → title-restate outro. 16:9. Free pipeline. `"register": "Pragmatist"`.

## The episodes (build in this order)

### E1 — "Why Stories Stick: Transformation, Not Résumé (framework primer)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a9-01-why-stories`
- **premise:** People forget bullet points and remember transformations. Before you write, learn
  the shapes that carry a change.
- **teach:** the core insight (it's about a transformation the reader sees themselves in); the
  **five frameworks** and their core movement — Hero's Journey (challenge → transformation →
  return with solution), The Quest (mission → obstacles → goal), Overcoming the Monster (threat →
  confrontation → victory → new order), Rags to Riches (humble start → opportunity → setback →
  growth → success), The Mentor (need → guidance → transformation → legacy); the path angles
  (A: pivotal moment / signature approach / shaping failure / your "why" / how the tool changed
  your work — B: origin / a user's pain / mission / a transformation it enables / the insight
  others missed). Pick **3 frameworks** + the angles for them.
- **traps:** confusing a résumé with a story; picking three frameworks that all feel the same.
- **deliverable:** three chosen frameworks + angles. Your Turn = "list your raw bullets for three
  different moments — one per framework you picked."

### E2 — "Write Three 100-Word Arcs in Three Frameworks (Part 1, 35 pts)" (~7 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a9-02-three-arcs`
- **premise:** Three tiny, deployable stories — each a standalone asset for your About page,
  LinkedIn, a bio, or a post.
- **teach:** for each of the three: **name the framework at the top**; hit its beginning/middle/end;
  pack in **concrete, specific details**; land at ~100 words; make it **publish-ready** and
  **human-sounding**. Show the Claude workflow: paste bullets → "structure these into a [Hero's
  Journey] arc, ~100 words, sound like a real person not a press release" → then the voice pass
  where the student swaps in their real words. Keep the three **distinct**.
- **traps:** unlabeled framework; generic statements; three near-identical stories; AI-slop voice.
- **deliverable:** three 100-word arcs. Your Turn = a prompt (one per framework) that structures
  the student's bullets into a 100-word arc to then rewrite in their voice.

### E3 — "Expand Your Strongest to 300 Words (Part 1, 25 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a9-03-expanded-story`
- **premise:** Your best arc, opened up into the story your About page actually needs.
- **teach:** pick the strongest arc; expand to **300 words** on the five-point structure — **Setup**
  (scene/challenge/opportunity), **Rising Action** (what happened, what was at stake), **Turning
  Point** (the decision/insight that changed everything), **Resolution** (what changed/what you
  created), **Takeaway** (what it means for the audience/users). Earned, specific, true — **your**
  voice, not a "brand voice."
- **traps:** missing the turning point; a takeaway that's about you not the reader; brand-voice
  cosplay instead of real voice.
- **deliverable:** the 300-word expanded story. Your Turn = a prompt that expands the chosen arc
  into the five-point structure from the student's material, then the voice pass.

### E4 — "Write the 500-Word Article (Part 2, ~25 of 40 pts)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a9-04-the-article`
- **premise:** Now the public piece — your strongest arc as the backbone of an article a stranger
  will actually read to the end.
- **teach:** build the **500+ word article** with the chosen framework **visible in the structure**;
  path A (lead with expertise + POV, weave the Madison tool in naturally, position as a
  practitioner worth following) or path B (make the reader care about the problem, build to the
  value prop, show before/after, end with a **clear CTA**); professional formatting — **headline,
  subheadings, short paragraphs, scannable**.
- **traps:** the arc invisible; a wall of text; no CTA; expertise buried.
- **deliverable:** the 500-word article draft. Your Turn = a prompt that turns the expanded story
  into a scannable 500-word article with headline + subheads, then the voice/CTA pass.

### E5 — "The Graphic, the Publish, and the Promo (Part 2, ~15 of 40 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a9-05-graphic-publish-promote`
- **premise:** An article nobody sees isn't published. Add a branded visual, put it live, and earn
  the click.
- **teach:** create **at least one custom graphic** (tool screenshot, branded image, process
  diagram, or before/after) in Canva / Adobe Express / Midjourney — on-brand, not generic;
  **publish live** on LinkedIn Articles / Medium / Substack and grab the **URL**; write the
  **LinkedIn promotion caption** — 2–3 sentences that earn the click (a hook, not a summary).
- **traps:** a generic stock image; "published" only as a PDF; a flat promo caption.
- **deliverable:** the live URL + graphic + promo post. Your Turn = a prompt that drafts a 2–3
  sentence LinkedIn caption with a hook from the article's turning point.

### E6 — "Assemble & Ship the PDF" (~4 min) — closer
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a9-06-assemble-submit`
- **premise:** One PDF, specific contents, easy points to lose on mechanics.
- **teach:** assemble the single **PDF** — the **three 100-word stories each with its framework
  labeled**, the **300-word expanded story**, the **clickable article URL**, a **screenshot of the
  LinkedIn promotion post**, **word counts on every piece**, and the **custom graphic visible**;
  name it **`LastName_FirstName_Assignment9.pdf`**; submit to Canvas. A final voice check: read one
  story aloud — does it sound like you?
- **deliverable:** the submitted PDF. Your Turn = "read your three stories to a friend without
  saying which framework each uses — if they can't feel the difference, that's your revision."
