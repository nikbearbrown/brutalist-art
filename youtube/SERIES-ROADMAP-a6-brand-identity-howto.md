## Honesty / craft threads (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW)

- **The gut-check test for the UVP.** Read it out loud — "would you say this in an interview
  without cringing?" If it sounds like a LinkedIn template, it's not done. Teach the test.
- **Generic brand = the top pitfall.** Elements that could apply to any professional score
  lowest. Specific-to-you/your-tool beats polished-but-empty every time.
- **Trademark + URL searches need REAL documentation** — screenshots of the actual Justia results
  and domain checks, not "I think it's free." Risk rated high/medium/low with evidence.
- **The Madison +1 is human judgment.** Submit BOTH the pipeline's draft Mission/UVP AND your
  rewrite, and explain what you changed and why — the "+1" the pipeline can't supply. This is the
  accountable-AI, human-in-the-loop theme again.
- **Portfolio analysis is specific or it's nothing** — named examples, screenshots, and a concrete
  "one thing I'd improve," not vibes.

## Series doctrine (every episode)

- **Assume zero brand-strategy experience.** Define every term first time (mission vs purpose vs
  vision vs values, UVP, brand pillar, positioning, competitive matrix, trademark, TLD). Never "just."
- **One deliverable per episode**, ending in the real artifact, with the paste-ready prompt (often
  a `nina`/`brandy` invocation) that drafts it — then the make-it-sound-like-you pass.
- **ai-explainer bookends stay:** composer cold open → step-by-step body → verdict recap → Your
  Turn → title-restate outro. 16:9. Free pipeline. `"register": "Pragmatist"`.

## The episodes (build in this order)

### E1 — "Brand Foundation: Mission, Purpose, Vision, Values + a UVP You'd Say Out Loud (Part 1A, 15 pts)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-01-brand-foundation`
- **premise:** Four short statements decide everything downstream — and one UVP you can say in an
  interview without cringing.
- **teach:** the four foundation statements — **Mission** (what: purpose/who you serve/how you
  create value), **Purpose** (why: the deeper motivation beyond money), **Vision** (where: the
  ultimate impact), **Values** (how: 3–5 you actually stand for). Then the **UVP** formula
  (personal: "I help [employers] achieve [result] through [unique skills]"; startup: "Our product
  helps [audience] achieve [result] through [offering]"); write **3–5 iterations** with
  refinements; the **gut-check read-aloud test**; final UVP + justification. Introduce `nina`.
- **traps:** generic statements; a UVP that sounds like a template; skipping the iterations.
- **deliverable:** the foundation + UVP development. Your Turn = `nina /uvp` (or a prompt) that
  drafts 5 UVP iterations from the Conductor Brief, then the gut-check pass.

### E2 — "Positioning & Pillars: What Sets You Apart (Part 1B, 15 pts)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-02-positioning-pillars`
- **premise:** Pillars are the handful of things you want people to remember — and a matrix that
  shows where you sit that nobody else does.
- **teach:** the **brand pillars** — Promise (what people can expect), Personality (3–5 traits),
  Story (background → audience need), Differentiation (what sets you apart), Experience (how people
  feel working with you/it). Then the **positioning analysis**: 3–5 professionals (personal) or
  competitors (startup); build a **competitive matrix** of strengths/approaches; highlight your
  unique position. Point at `brandy` for the audit/matrix.
- **traps:** pillars that fit anyone; superficial competitive analysis; no clear unique position.
- **deliverable:** pillars + positioning matrix. Your Turn = `brandy` on the target field (or a
  prompt) that builds the competitive matrix and names the white space.

### E3 — "Name It: Generate 10 (5 Descriptive + 5 Creative) (Part 2A, 10 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-03-name-generation`
- **premise:** A good name is discovered, not forced — generate wide first, judge later.
- **teach:** the split — **5 descriptive** names (clearly communicate function) + **5 creative/
  abstract** (distinctive, memorable); for each: a 2–3 sentence rationale, category, and an initial
  impression score (1–10). Use AI to generate from the tool's function, then the human taste pass.
  The name should reflect the Part 1 brand, not fight it.
- **traps:** ten variations of one idea; names disconnected from the positioning; no rationale.
- **deliverable:** 10 documented names. Your Turn = a prompt that generates 5 descriptive + 5
  creative names from the tool_summary with rationales and scores.

### E4 — "Validate the Name: Trademark Search on Justia (Part 2B, 10 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-04-trademark-search`
- **premise:** A name you can't legally use is worthless — check before you fall in love.
- **teach:** take your **top 5** to **trademarks.justia.com**; how to search; document per name —
  the search process (**screenshots**), results summary, potential conflicts, and a **risk rating
  (high/medium/low)** with reasoning; narrow to **3** based on availability. Not legal advice —
  it's a first-pass risk screen (say so honestly).
- **traps:** "I think it's available" with no screenshots; ignoring near-matches; skipping the risk rating.
- **deliverable:** trademark documentation → 3 names. Your Turn = "search your top 5 on Justia,
  screenshot each; here's a prompt that turns the results into a risk table."

### E5 — "Claim the Name: URL Availability + Final Pick (Part 2C, 10 pts)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-05-url-and-final-name`
- **premise:** The last gate — is the domain free, and does the name still hold up across
  trademark AND URL? Then commit.
- **teach:** check **`.com / .ai / .io / .app`** for the top 3; assess memorability/quality;
  consider alternate spellings; pick the **final name** on trademark + URL together; write the
  final recommendation + justification. The honest aside: **checking availability is free;
  actually buying the domain is a real-money decision — make it deliberately, not on impulse**
  (the buy_domain lesson).
- **traps:** picking a name whose domain is gone; ignoring the .ai/.io options; buying on a whim.
- **deliverable:** URL research + final name. Your Turn = a prompt that checks the shortlist across
  the four TLDs and drafts the final recommendation with trade-offs.

### E6 — "Portfolio Analysis: Steal Like a Pro (Part 3, 20 pts)" (~6 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-06-portfolio-analysis`
- **premise:** The fastest way to a great portfolio is studying 5–8 great ones — specifically,
  not vaguely.
- **teach:** research **5–8 portfolios** in the target field (personal: your desired role; startup:
  founder/team); per portfolio analyze structure, project showcasing, storytelling, technical
  demonstration, standout elements; for each, articulate **what you admire (with screenshots)**,
  why it's effective, how it could inspire yours, and **one thing you'd improve**; then a
  **best-practices synthesis**.
- **traps:** vague praise; no screenshots; no "what I'd change"; no synthesis.
- **deliverable:** portfolio analysis + synthesis. Your Turn = a prompt that structures the
  per-portfolio analysis and drafts the best-practices synthesis from the student's notes.

### E7 — "The Madison +1 & Ship It: PDF + Figma (touchpoint + submission)" (~5 min)
- **slug:** `claude-liam-a6-07-plus-one-and-submit`
- **premise:** Prove the human matters — then package it like a job application.
- **teach:** the **Madison +1** — run the recipe pipeline to draft **Mission + UVP**, then rewrite
  both in your own voice; submit **both versions** and explain in 2–3 sentences **what you changed
  and why** (the judgment the pipeline can't supply). Then assemble the **PDF** (<30MB: foundation,
  pillars/positioning, 10 names, trademark, URL, portfolio analysis, final name, +1 comparison —
  headers, page numbers, labeled screenshots); **update the Figma board** (final name, 1–2 sentence
  description, simplified competitive chart); submit **PDF to Canvas + Figma link in the comment**.
- **traps:** forgetting the Figma update (named pitfall); only submitting the pipeline OR your
  version of the +1; PDF over 30MB or poorly organized.
- **deliverable:** the PDF + updated Figma board. Your Turn = "read your final UVP out loud to
  someone — if they don't nod, that's your one rewrite before you export the PDF."
